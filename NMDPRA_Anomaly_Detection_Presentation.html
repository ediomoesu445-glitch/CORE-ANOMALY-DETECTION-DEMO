The Problem We Face Today

Every year, petroleum facilities fail —
and we often find out too late.

These failures are not random. They have warning signs. We are currently missing them.
💥

Equipment Failures

Pumps, compressors, heat exchangers and valves develop faults gradually. By the time operators notice, the damage is already done — shutdowns cost millions per day.

🔥

Fire & Explosion Risk

Undetected gas leaks, pressure build-ups and temperature spikes in LPG plants, refineries and pipelines can escalate into catastrophic fires in minutes.

🌊

Environmental Damage

Undetected pipeline leaks and tank overflows cause crude oil spills, contaminating farmland and waterways — costing the nation billions in cleanup and reputation.

👁️

Human Limitations

Operators monitor dozens of sensors simultaneously. Research shows humans can only reliably track 7 things at once. Modern facilities have 50–200 sensors per unit.

⚠️ The hard truth: In most petroleum facilities today, alarms only ring after a fault has already occurred. We react instead of prevent.

How the System Works

Three simple steps — fully automatic.

No complex training required for operators. The system does the thinking.
1
SENSE
The system reads all 52 facility sensors simultaneously — pressure, temperature, flow, valve positions — every few seconds, continuously.
2
ANALYSE
Two AI models instantly compare today's readings against what "normal" looks like. Any deviation — even invisible to humans — is flagged.
3
PINPOINT
When an alarm fires, the system shows exactly which sensor or which equipment is the root cause — no guesswork for engineers.
4
ALERT
A dashboard alarm is raised — red for confirmed fault, yellow for elevated risk — giving engineers time to act before things escalate.

🤖 Two AI layers work together: The first layer (MSPC) acts like a sensitive smoke detector — it flags any deviation from normal. The second layer (LightGBM AI) confirms the fault type and severity. Together they achieve 99.9% detection accuracy with almost zero false alarms.

✅ With the System

At 2:04 AM — 4 minutes after the leak starts — the AI detects unusual flow and pressure patterns across 3 sensors simultaneously. A dashboard alarm is raised. A maintenance crew is dispatched by 2:30 AM. Total loss: under 500 barrels. Crisis averted.

Real-Life NMDPRA Scenario — 2 of 4

🔥 LPG Plant
Overpressure Event

LPG bottling plants, gas processing facilities
Nigeria has over 3,000 licensed LPG plants. Overpressure in storage vessels is the leading cause of LPG explosions. A single cooling water pump failure can silently set off a chain reaction that ends in catastrophe within 30 minutes.

This is precisely the kind of fault our system detects — a cooling water temperature rising abnormally before pressure even reaches the danger zone.

❌ Without the System

A cooling water valve sticks at 11:00 PM. Temperature rises slowly. By 11:45 PM, vessel pressure reaches the safety relief threshold. The relief valve opens noisily — waking workers who then attempt a manual shutdown under pressure. A fire breaks out. Two workers are injured.

✅ With the System

At 11:03 PM — 3 minutes after the valve sticks — the AI detects an unusual cooling water outlet temperature pattern and raises an amber alert. The night supervisor is paged. The faulty valve is identified and manually corrected by 11:20 PM. No incident. No injury. No fine.

NMDPRA impact: Proactive safety regulation — the Authority can require all licensed LPG plants to run this system as a licence condition.

Real-Life NMDPRA Scenario — 3 of 4

🏭 Petroleum Depot
Storage Tank Anomaly

Atlas Cove, Mosimi, PPMC depots and licensed private depots
Floating roof tanks can develop seal failures, causing fuel vapour release and product loss. Level transmitter faults can cause overfilling — the same type of failure that caused the Buncefield oil terminal explosion in the UK (2005, ₦500bn damage).

Our system continuously monitors tank level, temperature, and inflow/outflow ratios — detecting discrepancies that signal a seal failure or instrument fault before overflow.

❌ Without the System

A faulty tank level sensor reads lower than actual. A pump continues filling the tank beyond capacity. Fuel overflows onto the bund area. An ignition source nearby causes a fire. The depot is shut for 6 weeks. NMDPRA issues a fine, but the damage is done.

✅ With the System

The AI notices the inflow rate and outflow rate don't match what the level sensor shows — a classic signature of a faulty instrument. It raises an alert: "Tank 5 — Level Sensor Discrepancy." The filling pump is stopped remotely. Sensor is replaced the next morning. No spill. No shutdown.

NMDPRA impact: AI-generated evidence that a company ignored a flagged instrument fault becomes a clear basis for regulatory enforcement action.

Real-Life NMDPRA Scenario — 4 of 4

⚙️ Gas Plant
Compressor Fault

Gas processing plants, NGL recovery facilities, export terminals
Compressors are the heart of gas processing plants — and among the most expensive equipment to repair or replace. A single centrifugal compressor can cost $5 million to replace. Bearing wear, impeller fouling, and seal degradation all develop gradually over weeks before catastrophic failure.

Our system detects these signatures early — giving engineers weeks of advance warning, not minutes.

❌ Without the System

A compressor bearing begins degrading in January. By March, the compressor seizes, causing an unplanned plant shutdown. The facility loses 45 days of production. Emergency part procurement from overseas takes 3 weeks. Total cost: $12 million in lost production + $4 million repair.

✅ With the System

In mid-January, the AI flags subtle changes in compressor suction pressure and discharge temperature — the early fingerprint of bearing wear. Engineers schedule a planned maintenance shutdown in February — 3 days, bearing replaced at $200,000. The $16 million disaster is avoided.

NMDPRA impact: Fewer unplanned shutdowns means more consistent gas supply, less gas flaring, and lower carbon emissions — all within NMDPRA's mandate.
